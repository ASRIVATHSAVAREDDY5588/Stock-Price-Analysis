Value at Risk (VaR) & Expected Shortfall

Value at Risk (VaR) at confidence level 1 - α is the threshold loss such that losses exceed this level only with probability α.

Expected Shortfall (CVaR) is the conditional expectation of loss given that the loss has exceeded the VaR threshold: ES_α = -E[R | R ≤ -VaR_α]. It quantifies how severe tail losses average when a crash occurs.

Skewness & Excess Kurtosis

Skewness (3rd standardized moment): Measures distributional asymmetry. Negative skewness denotes frequent small gains and occasional large, sharp market crashes (crash asymmetry).

Excess Kurtosis (4th standardized moment - 3): Measures tail thickness relative to a Normal distribution (where kurtosis = 0). Values > 0 indicate a leptokurtic distribution with heavy tails.

Student-t vs Gaussian Modeling

The Student-t distribution features an additional shape parameter: degrees of freedom (ν).

  • When ν ≤ 5, tails are thick and standard Gaussian VaR severely underestimates risk.
  • When ν > 30, the Student-t distribution converges asymptotically to the Normal distribution.

The Central Limit Theorem (CLT)

The Central Limit Theorem establishes that the sum or average of independent random variables with finite variance converges to a Normal distribution as sample size n → ∞, regardless of the underlying population's shape.

IID vs Moving-Block Bootstrap

IID Bootstrap: Draws single days with replacement. Assumes returns are independent.

Moving Block Bootstrap: Draws contiguous chunks of consecutive trading days (e.g., 5 or 10 days). Preserves serial autocorrelation and volatility clustering.
